01

OrderKhaja

QR menu & table ordering for cafés and restaurants

Live · Subscription

The problem

Cafés taking orders by phone and paper ticket lose track of tables, mishear orders and guess their numbers at the end of the day.

OrderKhaja lets any café sign up and go live in minutes: guests scan the QR on their table and order from their phone — no app to install — while the owner runs everything from one dashboard.

What I built

  • Multi-tenant architecture with PostgreSQL row-level security, so every restaurant’s data stays isolated
  • QR table ordering, live order tracking, and menu & table management
  • Subscription billing with trial expiry and eSewa, Nepal’s local payment gateway
  • Daily revenue summaries for owners
Role
Product design & full-stack development

Next.js, Supabase, PostgreSQL, Tailwind CSS

02

PulseMart

Self-hosted e-commerce with an AI-powered admin

Available · One-time licence

Small online stores usually choose between paying heavily for a hosted platform or hand-rolling something fragile. PulseMart is a complete, self-hosted alternative — with an admin assistant that answers plain-English questions about orders and inventory, but never changes anything without sign-off.

What I built

  • Catalog, cart and checkout with online and cash-on-delivery payment, plus order tracking
  • Email-OTP verified accounts, JWT sessions and self-service password reset
  • AI admin assistant with a confirm-before-write flow and a full audit log
Stack
Next.js, React, Node.js, Express, PostgreSQL, Tailwind CSS
Model
From $249, one-time — source code plus personal setup help

Flavoro

Online food ordering & restaurant management

Private codebase

Restaurants juggling phone orders and paper tickets lose track of stock and money fast. Flavoro brings it into one system: customers order online, staff work within role-based permissions, and owners see their finances in real time — in an interface built for the phone in their hand.

What I built

  • Core backend modules for inventory management and real-time financial tracking
  • Role-based access control for owners and staff
  • A modern, mobile-responsive interface for customers and staff
Stack
PHP, MySQL, HTML, CSS, Tailwind CSS, Bootstrap
Access
Not open source — walkthrough available on request

I’m drawn to the unglamorous problems that decide whether a small business has a good day: the order that got misheard, the stock count nobody updated, the bill that doesn’t add up. Principles, and where they show up

  1. i.

    Isolation by default

    Every restaurant on OrderKhaja shares one database, so separation is enforced at the database itself with row-level security policies — not left to application code to remember.

    In OrderKhaja

  2. ii.

    AI proposes, people approve

    PulseMart’s assistant can read the store and suggest changes, but nothing is written until an admin confirms — and every action lands in an audit log.

    In PulseMart

  3. iii.

    Built for the people at the counter

    Staff get exactly the permissions their role needs, owners see the money in real time, and every screen works on the phone already in their pocket.

    In Flavoro

  4. iv.

    Made for where it’s used

    Payments through eSewa, prices in rupees, and guests who order without installing anything — local details decide whether software gets adopted.

    In OrderKhaja
